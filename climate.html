Climate Zones & Biomes

Due to its extreme length from north to south, Japan's climate ranges dramatically from the humid continental climate of Hokkaido in the north to the humid subtropical and tropical climates of Okinawa in the south. This vast latitudinal spread creates incredibly diverse biomes, ranging from subalpine coniferous forests to dense subtropical rainforests.

Typhoons

Japan is frequently struck by typhoons in late summer and early autumn. These massive storm systems bring extreme winds and torrential rains, significantly impacting both human settlements and the physical geography through erosion.

Flora & Fauna

Because of its isolation from the mainland, Japan has a high degree of endemism. Native wildlife includes the Japanese macaque (snow monkey), the sika deer, and the red-crowned crane. Forests cover over 60% of the landmass.

The Four Seasons

The changing of the four distinct seasons (Shiki) is a fundamental aspect of Japanese culture, aesthetics, and poetry.

Spring (Haru)

Defined by the brief, spectacular blooming of the sakura (cherry blossoms), moving in a wave from south to north.

Summer (Natsu)

Hot and highly humid, beginning with a rainy season (tsuyu) and characterized by lush, vibrant green forests.

Autumn (Aki)

Famous for 'koyo', the vibrant changing of the autumn foliage, where maple leaves turn deep, fiery reds and golds.

Winter (Fuyu)

Cold and crisp. The western coast and northern regions experience "ocean-effect" snow, creating some of the heaviest snowfalls in the world.
