GEOGRAPHY OF JAPAN

Discover Japan

An Island Nation

East Asia's Archipelago

Japan is an island country located in East Asia, situated in the northwest Pacific Ocean. It is bordered on the west by the Sea of Japan, and extends from the Sea of Okhotsk in the north toward the East China Sea and Taiwan in the south.

The archipelago consists of thousands of islands, but the geography is dominated by four main islands: Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu. These islands form a crescent shape off the eastern coast of the Asian continent.

Japan's landscape is highly rugged, with over 70% of the land covered by mountains and heavily forested areas. This unique geography has profoundly shaped the nation's culture, economy, and history, fostering a deep respect and spiritual connection with nature, famously seen in Shinto traditions.

Key Geographic Features

Mount Fuji

An iconic stratovolcano and Japan's highest peak, revered culturally and recognized worldwide for its exceptionally symmetrical cone.

Japanese Alps

A series of mountain ranges bisecting the main island of Honshu, offering dramatic alpine scenery and shaping regional climates.

Ring of Fire

Situated on the Pacific Ring of Fire, Japan experiences immense tectonic activity, resulting in numerous volcanoes, earthquakes, and hot springs.

Kuroshio Current

A strong western boundary current in the Pacific Ocean that warms Japan's southern coastlines and enriches its marine biodiversity.

Sakura Ecosystems

The famous cherry blossoms are just one part of Japan's diverse temperate and subtropical forest ecosystems, changing dramatically with the four distinct seasons.

Typhoons

The late summer and early autumn bring intense tropical cyclones that deliver massive rainfall, shaping the erosion and fluvial systems of the islands.
