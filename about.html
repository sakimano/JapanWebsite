About Japan

Location & Demographics

Coordinates: Roughly 36°N Latitude, 138°E Longitude.

Japan is located off the eastern coast of the Asian continent, stretching from the Sea of Okhotsk in the north to the East China Sea in the south.

Population: Approximately 125 million people.

Due to the heavily mountainous terrain, the population is densely concentrated in narrow coastal plains. Over 90% of the population is urban.

Major Cities: Tokyo (the capital and largest metropolitan economy in the world), Yokohama, Osaka, Nagoya, and Sapporo.

History & Culture

Historical Timeline

Japan's history spans from the prehistoric Jōmon period (c. 14,000 BCE) known for early pottery, through the classical Heian period of cultural flourishing, the feudal era of the samurai and shogunate, and into the modern post-Meiji Restoration era which rapidly transformed Japan into an industrial power.

The Ainu Culture

The Ainu are the indigenous people of the lands surrounding the Sea of Okhotsk, including Hokkaido island. Historically hunter-gatherers, they have a distinct language, culture, and animistic religion deeply tied to the natural geography and wildlife, particularly the brown bear.

Isolation & Development

Japan's island geography historically afforded it natural protection from mainland invasions. During the Edo period (1603–1867), Japan implemented a strict isolationist policy (Sakoku), which allowed a unique, deeply unified culture and artistic tradition—like Ukiyo-e—to flourish without heavy outside influence.

Cartography

Mapping the Archipelago

The mapping of Japan has a rich history, deeply tied to both administrative needs and cultural identity. Traditional Japanese maps often lacked modern strict scale and orientation (north wasn't always up), instead depicting space relationally, emphasizing important roads, temples, and coastal routes.

In the 19th century, cartographer Inō Tadataka spent 17 years walking the coastline of Japan to produce the Dai Nihon Enkai Yochi Zenzu, the first map of Japan using modern surveying techniques. His work accurately captured the complex coastline and thousands of islands that define the nation's geography.
